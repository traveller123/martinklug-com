I use AI every day and it has changed almost nothing about how I make decisions.

It writes my first drafts. It reads documents I would otherwise have skimmed. It gets me from a blank page to something I can react to, which has always been the expensive part for me.

What it has not done is make a single hard call easier.

The difficult ones this year were all the same shape. Whether to commit capital to something. Whether a person was the right person. Whether to keep going with something that was working, slowly. None of them were short of information. I could have generated twenty pages of analysis on each. In two cases I did.

What each one actually needed was somebody willing to be accountable for a call that could not be fully justified in advance.

## The founders who could decide early

As an angel investor I have watched companies from the earliest stages. I have seen brilliant people flame out in months, and unremarkable-sounding ideas turn into real businesses. The difference was never the idea. It was not market timing, the pitch deck or the pedigree. It was how long the founder could sit with ambiguity and still make decisions.

The ones who needed certainty before acting stalled. Every decision became a research project. Every risk became a reason to pause. The ones who could move on 60% of the information and adjust as they went built something.

Corporate trains you to gather evidence until the decision is obvious. That works when the downside of a wrong call is a bad quarterly review. It does not work when the cost of waiting is running out of runway, or motivation.

## Why the tool makes this harder, not easier

A model will give a fluent answer to anything, immediately, in a confident register. For a certain kind of person (analytical, careful, trained by a large organisation to be right before moving) that is the most comfortable procrastination ever invented. It is possible to spend a full week feeling productive and end up precisely where you started.

I still use it constantly. I have one rule: I do not ask it about something I already know the answer to and am avoiding.

AI removed the cost of producing analysis. It did not remove the cost of deciding. That cost was always the one that mattered.
